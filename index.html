How it runs this fast

Synthetic panels, built from real, current evidence.

The speed comes from the panel. The thinking is still done the long way.

Real people, anonymized

Each respondent starts from a real, anonymized US Census household record, drawn in proportion to how common people like them are among the buyers you screen for. Your buyers, rather than the general population.

Current attitudes, with sources

Attitudes come from a scan of current public evidence, run fresh for every study and weighted to the past twelve months. Each one carries the source it came from and the date it was retrieved.

Interviewed, not surveyed

A moderator interviews each respondent for ninety minutes and keeps probing past the first answer, because the first answer a person gives is usually a rationalization. A stated reason never counts as a finding.

Why the work holds up

Experience is knowing what good looks like, and making sure every study gets there.

Twenty years teaches you the handful of places research goes soft, and how to keep it from happening. Every study is checked against those points before it reaches you, and the results go in the deliverable.

DepthWhether the moderator probed a must-cover topic or accepted the easy answer and moved on.

AgreementWhether respondents pushed back on the moderator's framing or simply adopted it.

Invented depthWhether a motivation a respondent stated rests on something real in their profile.

TraceabilityWhether every claim in the analysis points at an interview turn that says it.

StabilityA share of interviews is rerun, to confirm the same people hold the same positions.

CompositionHow the panel that was built compares against the screener it was built to meet.

Straight about the method

Directional, and it says so on the page.

This is qualitative work, and it does what excellent qualitative work does. It tells you why people choose what they choose, and what would change their minds. It gives you a direction and the reasoning behind it.

Synthetic respondents model patterns of reasoning rather than individual people, so findings are directional and are never presented as projectable. Before a major spend, confirm the direction with a small live check.

Every deliverable states its method, its panel, its sources and its limits, right where your stakeholders can read them. Work that is straight about what it is tends to get trusted with the next decision.

Questions

What people ask before commissioning a project.

Is this real research, or is it AI making things up?

Every respondent starts from a real, anonymized US Census household record, and every attitude they hold comes from a scan of current public evidence that carries its source and the date it was retrieved. What is synthetic is the conversation, not the evidence underneath it.

Nothing reaches your deck on the model's word. Quotes are pulled out of the transcript by turn number, so a quote cannot drift from what was said, and a claim that cannot be traced back to a turn is dropped before you see it.

How is this different from asking ChatGPT what my customers think?

A chatbot gives you the average of everything ever written about your category. It is confident, it is fluent, and it tells you what you already know.

This builds a panel of specific people who disagree with each other, screened to your actual buyers, then interviews each one for ninety minutes and reports where they split. The disagreement is the finding. An average cannot produce one.

Where do the respondents come from?

From Census household records, drawn in proportion to how common people like them are among the buyers your screener describes. Your buyers, not the general population. A study on premium dog food is not full of people who do not own a dog.

Each person gets a life that fits their record: work, household, money, routines, what else they buy in the category and what they have never tried. You review the panel before anyone is interviewed.

How do you know they are not all the same person underneath?

Because a panel that agrees with itself is worthless, and it is the failure this method has to be built against.

Every study works out how buyers in that category genuinely divide, from that category's own evidence, and each respondent is built on a position, including what they do not believe. Somebody built to walk away over price does not drift into saying the cheap one is good enough. How far the panel agrees is measured and reported in the deliverable.

Can I see the transcripts?

Yes. The full transcripts come with the study, along with the discussion guide, the screener and every panel profile. Any number in the deck can be followed back to the interviews behind it, and any quote to the moment it was said.

What can this not do?

It will not give you a projectable number, and it is not the instrument for sizing a market or setting a forecast. Findings are directional, and they say so on the page.

Before a major spend, confirm the direction with a small live check. If your question needs a number rather than a reason, you will hear that in the first reply rather than after you have paid for a study.

What do you need from me, and how long does it take?

A paragraph on the decision you are trying to make is enough to start. Four working days from approval to presentation, and you sit at two points in it: the discussion guide on day one and the panel on day two.

Between those, the work runs. You are not managing a fieldwork vendor or waiting on a recruiter.

What happens to my brief and my data?

Your brief is used to run your study and nothing else. It is never used to train a model, and it is not pooled with anyone else's work.

Your name is not used as a reference without your say-so, and a case study only happens if you agree to it in writing.
